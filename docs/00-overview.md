The **Sonic Physics Guide** is comprehensive resource, dedicated to describing the inner workings of the classic [Mega Drive](https://info.sonicretro.org/Mega_Drive) & [Mega CD](https://info.sonicretro.org/Mega_CD) [Sonic](https://info.sonicretro.org/Sonic) games ([Sonic 1](https://info.sonicretro.org/Sonic_1), [Sonic 2](https://info.sonicretro.org/Sonic_2), [Sonic CD](https://info.sonicretro.org/Sonic_CD), and [Sonic 3 & Knuckles](https://info.sonicretro.org/Sonic_3_%26_Knuckles)) *(along with [Sonic Mania](https://info.sonicretro.org/Sonic_Mania))*, Going over the values, rules, rules governing player movement, slope physics, [Badnik](https://info.sonicretro.org/Badnik)/object behavior, construction of the sloped terrain, and much more! The guide sheds light on exactly how these aspects contribute to the gameplay.

> [!NOTE]
> Unless explicitly stated, the following research applies to all 5 original titles.

Whether you're a curious newcomer, experienced [fan game](https://info.sonicretro.org/Fan_game) developer, or a seasoned Sonic fan seeking a deeper understanding of the games, this guide aims to present the information in an accessible manner, providing concise breakdowns of underlying concepts.

This guide is engine-agnostic, explaining the game logic in general terms rather than going into the fullest details of the original [m68k assembly](https://info.sonicretro.org/Motorola_68000). As such, the information can be used in many programming languages, (Python, C++, Fortran, etc.) and game engines/IDE's, (GameMaker, Clickteam, Godot, etc.).

> [!NOTE]
> The guide will still acknowledge the quirks and workarounds the original games used due to hardware limitations.

You can find the table of contents on the righthand side of every page, it's recommended that you read these pages in order, as many of them use concepts explained in previous pages.
